## What the counts show, and premises that do not hold

- The prediction was that intact fused ODC halves occur only where there is no intact standalone ODC. Of the 10 species with an intact fused half in Test A, 6 do have an intact standalone ODC.
- *Gracilariopsis chorda* does not carry an intact standalone ODC by this task's rule (>= 11/13): its only ODC-family protein, A0A2V3IYX3, is 10 kept / 1 lost (C360R) / 2 unresolved. So the statement that all four degraded-fusion species carry intact standalone ODCs does not hold; three do (Galdieria partita at 11/13, Percolomonas at 12/13 by sequence, Rhodosorus at 13/13).
- The green-lineage fusions are not all intact: p9 leaves 5 fusion species undetermined (listed above), and they are excluded, not counted as intact.
- Only 4 species have a degraded fused half, so neither test can detect anything but a very strong association; p = 1 here means no association was detected, not that none exists.
- Proteome information comes from the UniProt proteomes API (cached in data/p19/); data/q_proteomes/ holds only six FASTA files and no completeness data.
- Short alignments: identities computed over fewer than 100 aligned residues are unreliable (Adiantum A0A9D4ZKC7 0.381 over 42, Tetraselmis A0A6T8DES7 0.333 over 48, Pycnococcus UPI003D4D0C8E 0.244 over 41, Auxenochlorella A0A1D2A4Z4 0.224 over 58). None changes a species call: a fragment that short cannot cover 11 of the 13 sites, so it could not count as an intact ODC either way.
- Sites were read from structure only for the five red-algal proteins with a model already in data/p10_models/; every green-lineage protein was read by sequence.
