## Gracilariopsis chorda: one standalone ODC-family protein or three?

- **A0A2V3IYX3** (433 aa; UniProt name "ornithine decarboxylase"): 45.7 % identical to human ODC over 372 aligned residues; position C360 = **R** (protein residue 380).
- **A0A2V3IKW4** (472 aa; UniProt name "Diaminopimelate decarboxylase"): 28.5 % identical to human ODC over 344 aligned residues; position C360 = **C** (protein residue 400).
- **A0A2V3IW11** (427 aa; UniProt name "Putative diaminopimelate decarboxylase, chloroplastic"): 27.2 % identical to human ODC over 368 aligned residues; position C360 = **C** (protein residue 356).

**Which is right.** Both statements count different things, and both are correct for what they count:

- `p7_same_species.tsv` lists **three** standalone proteins because it counts every protein with a PLP-decarboxylase domain (PF02784) and no AdoMetDC domain ("decarboxylase only"). That column does not say ODC.
- Only **one** of the three, A0A2V3IYX3, is ODC-family: it is 45.7 % identical to human ODC, in the same range as the Galdieria and Rhodosorus ODC-family proteins in the table above (45-48 %), and UniProt names it ornithine decarboxylase. A0A2V3IKW4 and A0A2V3IW11 are 27-28 % identical to human ODC, in the range of the proteins above that UniProt or p10 call diaminopimelate decarboxylase (25-28 %), and UniProt names both diaminopimelate decarboxylase.
- So the earlier note is right if read as "one standalone **ODC-family** protein", and that protein has **R at C360** by this sequence alignment (as in p10). The other two have **C at C360**, but they are not ODC-family, and these 13 positions alone do not show what they decarboxylate.
