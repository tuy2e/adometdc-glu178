## Chunk 1A verdict (so far)
- **Not found anywhere:**
  - a stramenopile-, cryptophyte- or euglenozoan-wide loss of Glu178;
  - a tree of class-2 AdoMetDC *within* eukaryotes;
  - a report of class-2 AdoMetDC moving between eukaryotic lineages;
  - the Ser109→Trp swap.
- **Closest neighbour: Li et al. 2026 (Michael lab, *JBC*).** It traces the *partner* enzymes (spermine/thermospermine synthases) across the same lineages we use (cryptophytes, euglenozoans, dinoflagellates, haptophytes) and finds gene transfer. They don't build an AdoMetDC tree and don't look at the pocket. We must cite it. It also means this lab is the group most likely to publish the AdoMetDC version, so timing matters.
- ✅ **Overlap resolved: no overlap.** Fan et al. 2020 (*Sci Adv*) lists 'S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase' as a gene diatoms acquired from bacteria. Their Data file S3 shows which genes:
  - five *Fragilariopsis* genes (Fracy1_145312, _155369, _155374, _155378, _165245) and one *Thalassiosira* gene (EED92531);
  - all short (~80 aa), with closest relatives in bacteria (Alphaproteobacteria, *Desulfarculus*, *Cyanobacterium*);
  - the *Thalassiosira* one is UniProt B8C3B1: 80 aa, Pfam **PF02675**.

  So these are the **bacterial-type (class-1b)** AdoMetDC family that Michael 2011 describes. They are not our class-2 PF01536 enzyme (~370 aa), which isn't in their list. Their method only flags genes that sit *inside* bacterial clades, which our enzyme can't do.
- **Correction to the WOW ranking:** Li et al. 2019 (*Biochem J*) already reported eukaryote-type (class-2) AdoMetDC in bacteria, acquired by gene transfer. So WOW idea #3 is **pre-empted**. That doesn't affect the stramenopile project.

Also PubMed for any measured AdoMetDC putrescine response in oomycetes, diatoms, brown algae, *Blastocystis*/thraustochytrids, cryptophytes, euglenids, slime moulds, *Dictyostelium* and parasitic protists.
- **Nothing on Ser109→Trp or an Asn at 178 in any organism.**
- **Glu178 has only been studied in human mutants**, plus one side note: Singh 2013 (*PLoS One*, [PMC3686867](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3686867/)) says *Leishmania* AdoMetDC shares 2 of 3 human putrescine-binding residues. That's consistent with our kinetoplastid Ser.
- **Classification:** Bale & Ealick 2010 ([PMC2847442](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2847442/)) define 2a (plants), 2b-I (putrescine-activated; conserved Lys80) and 2b-II (trypanosomatid prozyme). **There is no class for stramenopiles or cryptophytes**, so our data could define one.
  - New check: Lys80 is kept in stramenopiles and the dinoflagellate/*Perkinsus* copy, but cryptophytes have **Leu80** and kinetoplastids/euglenid **Ile80**.
- ***Plasmodium*:** Wells 2006 (*J Mol Graph Model*, PMID [16257247](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16257247/)) explains the missing putrescine response by built-in Arg11/Lys15/Lys215, a plant-like mechanism. Our mapping gives Arg at the human-L13 position, which matches.
- **Measured putrescine response in our Glu-less lineage:** only trypanosomatids (activated, mainly through the prozyme) and possibly ***Euglena gracilis*** (Aleksijevic 1979, abstract vague). **No stramenopile, oomycete or cryptophyte AdoMetDC has ever been tested**, so the experiment is open.
  - ✅ **Update 9 Nov (full text read):** *Euglena gracilis* AdoMetDC **is** putrescine-activated, 3–10× (Aleksijevic 1979). See the notes below. **Stramenopiles, oomycetes and cryptophytes are still untested.**

### ✅ Michael 2017 *Biochem J* 474:2277–2299, "Evolution of biosynthetic diversity" (PMID 28655863), 23 pp. **Verdict: does not pre-empt us.**
- **What it covers:** gene duplication, horizontal and endosymbiotic gene transfer, gene fusion and convergence, using polyamine pathways as the example.
- **What it says about AdoMetDC** (all of it):
  - Kinetoplastid **prozyme** is a dead AdoMetDC paralogue that activates AdoMetDC (Willert 2007, Volkov 2016). It may make up for kinetoplastids lacking the uORF translational control that mammals and plants use.
  - The eukaryotic (class-2) AdoMetDC is a **fusion of two bacterial class-1b genes**, seen only from the structure (Toms 2004). It "probably occurred prior to the last eukaryotic common ancestor, since it is found in all eukaryotic lineages."
  - A few class-2 homologues in γ-proteobacteria (e.g. *Shewanella*) were "likely" acquired from a eukaryotic donor. **No tree or donor is shown.**
  - Ciliates (*Tetrahymena*) replaced their eukaryotic AdoMetDC with a bacterial class-1b–spermidine-synthase fusion. *Paramecium* lost both genes. Diatoms carry extra class-1b fusions with signal peptides, probably for the long-chain silica polyamines (Michael 2011).
  - Animal spermine synthase has a dead class-1b domain (from bacteria, around the origin of animals).
  - Class-2 AdoMetDC is fused to ODC in *Plasmodium* and in the green alga ***Micromonas*** (Green 2011).
- **What it does NOT contain:**
  - no class-2 AdoMetDC tree among eukaryotes;
  - no class-2 gene transfer between eukaryotes;
  - no putrescine site, Glu178 or activation by putrescine;
  - no stramenopile, oomycete, cryptophyte or euglenid AdoMetDC (these groups appear only in the plastid-history and thermospermine paragraphs);
  - no dinoflagellate ODC–AdoMetDC fusion.
- **Useful for our paper:**
  1. **Cite it** for the "class 2 is ancestral to eukaryotes / in all lineages" baseline. Our result is a twist on that picture: one class-2 lineage crosses supergroups.
  2. **Its gene-transfer caution:** bacterial-looking genes in eukaryotic genomes are often assembly or contamination artifacts (Ku & Martin 2016 "70% rule"; Artamonova 2015). That backs up our pre-set contamination screen. We should cite those two in Methods. *Neither is in our pool yet; DOIs to be pulled from PubMed before citing.*
  3. **The *Shewanella* claim gives us a cheap side check:** do the bacterial class-2 enzymes that lack Glu178 (12/54 in the FOLDTRACE run) fall inside our Glu-less lineage? *(Not run; idea only.)*
- **Leads from its references:** Green et al. 2011 *Mol Microbiol* (PMID 21762220, the *Micromonas* fusion) is in our pool but has **not been read in full**. Added to the to-read list. Everything else AdoMetDC-related in its reference list is already in our pool.

### ✅ Michael 2016 *Biochem J* 473:2315–2329, "Biosynthesis of polyamines and polyamine-containing molecules" (PMID 27470594), 15 pp. **Verdict: does not pre-empt us.**
*(Two-column layout, so I extracted each column separately to read it in the right order. Viewed Table 2 as an image.)*
- **What it covers:** every route to diamines, triamines and tetra-amines in bacteria, archaea and eukaryotes, and how polyamines are built into other molecules. Ends with a short "evolutionary considerations" section.
- **What it says about AdoMetDC** (all of it):
  - Three forms share one origin: class 1a (bacteria, mainly γ-proteobacteria), class 1b (bacteria and archaea), and class 2 (II), "found mostly in eukaryotes". Table 2 uses human P17707 (334 aa, PDB 1JEN) as the class-2 example.
  - Class 2 = two class-1b enzymes fused (Toms 2004 vs Ekstrom 1999). It is "mostly absent from bacteria and archaea except for homologues in some *Shewanella* species, probably obtained horizontally". **This cites Illingworth 2003 (ref 84).**
  - It is "found in almost all eukaryotes that synthesize Spd" and "in all eukaryotic supergroups", so the fusion probably happened before the last eukaryotic common ancestor.
  - **Regulation:**
    - Mammals and plants control AdoMetDC through upstream ORFs in the mRNA. Budding yeast AdoMetDC seems insensitive to polyamine levels.
    - *T. brucei* has no such 5′ mRNA features and uses prozyme instead; prozyme raises activity "by orders of magnitude".
    - **Putrescine activation of AdoMetDC is never mentioned.**
  - The crenarchaeal class-1b paralogue became an arginine decarboxylase. The *Plasmodium* AdoMetDC–ODC fusion is mentioned (not *Micromonas* this time). The diatom AdoMetDC/aminopropyltransferase/SET mosaic proteins are mentioned (Michael 2011).
- **What it does NOT contain:**
  - no class-2 tree;
  - no eukaryote-to-eukaryote AdoMetDC transfer;
  - no putrescine site or Glu178;
  - no stramenopile/oomycete/cryptophyte/euglenid AdoMetDC (diatoms appear only for the long-chain polyamine fusions and thermospermine).
- **Useful for our paper:**
  1. **Cite it** together with Michael 2017 for the textbook view: class 2 is in all supergroups and was vertically inherited from before the last eukaryotic common ancestor. Our tree adds that one class-2 *gene lineage* now crosses supergroups.
  2. Its line that **kinetoplastids lack mRNA-based AdoMetDC control** is worth a sentence in our discussion: kinetoplastids are one of our Glu-less groups and regulate AdoMetDC differently (prozyme). *For stramenopiles, Michael 2011 (already read) notes short upstream ORFs in the* Thalassiosira *and* Phaeodactylum *AdoMetDC mRNAs, so diatoms probably do have this control. Cryptophytes and euglenids: not checked.*
- **New lead that matters:** **Illingworth et al. 2003 *FEBS Lett*** (PMID 12914918) is the source for the *Shewanella* class-2 gene-transfer claim, so it **may contain a class-2 tree with eukaryotes in it**. It's in our pool (Tier 3, 🟢 free, bronze) but **not read in full yet. Moving it up to Tier 1.**

### ✅ Li et al. 2015 *Mol Microbiol* 97:791–807, ciliates *Tetrahymena* and *Paramecium* (PMID 25994085), 17 pp. **Verdict: does not pre-empt us.**
*(Read the full text in order. Figures viewed: Fig. 3 tree and Table 1. Fig. 2 and Figs 4–9 read from their legends and the text. The only supplement is Fig. S1, an LC-MS control.)*
- **What it shows:**
  - Both ciliates **lost** the eukaryotic class-2 AdoMetDC and spermidine synthase.
  - *Tetrahymena* replaced them with a bacterial class-1b AdoMetDC–spermidine-synthase fusion, in 3 copies. Only a mix of two copies (Tet1+Tet2) is active, through a shared active site (homology model on *T. maritima* 3IWC).
  - *Paramecium* has a bacterial homospermidine synthase instead and accumulates homospermidine. Yeast can grow on homospermidine.
  - **Table 1** lists which polyamine genes are present in parasites (presence/absence only, no residues). It includes *T. cruzi* AdoMetDC EKG05581 and *L. donovani* XP_003863026.
- **The only tree is for spermidine synthase, not AdoMetDC** (Fig. 3: neighbour-joining, PAUP*, ClustalX, 1,000 bootstraps). In it:
  - The eukaryotic SpdSyn clade is one group. The ciliate fusions sit among bacteria (support 52).
  - ⚠️ **Inside the eukaryotes:**
    - the cryptophyte ***Guillardia* SpdSyn groups with the kinetoplastids** (*T. cruzi*, *T. brucei*, *L. donovani*);
    - the **stramenopiles group with red algae**, with the haptophyte *Emiliania* nested among them;
    - *Perkinsus* groups with *Plasmodium*.
  - **No support values are shown for any of these nodes.** The only numbers are 71 at a deep node, and 72 and 52 elsewhere. The paper says nothing about these groupings.
- **What it does NOT contain:**
  - no class-2 AdoMetDC tree or residue analysis;
  - no putrescine site or Glu178 (the "missing glutamate" in Tet1 is a class-1b *ribose-binding* Glu, not our site);
  - no gene transfer of class-2 AdoMetDC.
- **Useful for our paper:**
  1. **Cite it** for: ciliates have no class-2 AdoMetDC, which explains why our survey has none (matches our step 2 note); and Table 1 parasite gene losses.
  2. 💡 **New idea from Fig. 3 (my suggestion, not tested):** the partner enzyme spermidine synthase may also put a cryptophyte next to kinetoplastids. If spermidine synthase follows our AdoMetDC lineage, the two genes may have **travelled together**. If it doesn't, AdoMetDC moved alone. Either answer is informative.
     - **Caveats:** their tree is neighbour-joining with no support shown at that node, and it has one cryptophyte. In their tree **stramenopiles do *not* join that group** (they sit with red algae, which fits the red-algal endosymbiosis). So this is only a hint.
     - It would be a separate, contained analysis: same pipeline, spermidine synthase from the same organisms. **Logging it as possible chunk 2D; I won't start it unless you say so.**

### ✅ Fuell et al. 2010 *Plant Physiol Biochem* 48:513–520, "Polyamine biosynthetic diversity in plants and algae" (PMID 20227886), 8 pp. **Verdict: does not pre-empt us.**
*(Read the full text in order, plus the reference list. Fig. 1 is a pathway sketch and Fig. 2 shows siderophore/amide structures; neither is about AdoMetDC.)*
- **What it covers:**
  - plant ODC and the arginine route (from the cyanobacterial ancestor of the chloroplast);
  - AdoMetDC, spermidine, spermine and thermospermine synthases;
  - unusual polyamines and polyamine-based plant chemicals.
  - "Algae" here means **green algae only**. No stramenopile, cryptophyte, haptophyte or red-algal AdoMetDC is discussed.
- **What it says about AdoMetDC** (one paragraph, §2.3.1):
  - Mammalian AdoMetDC processing and activity are increased by putrescine.
  - **Carnation and potato** AdoMetDC processing is fast and **not** enhanced by putrescine (Lee 1997; Xiong 1997). Potato AdoMetDC is fully active without putrescine and works as a monomer (Bennett 2002).
  - Plant and mammalian AdoMetDC mRNAs are under translational control by spermidine/spermine. In plants that is the main feedback point, since plants have no antizyme.
  - "Like almost all eukaryotes, all plants and algae possess AdoMetDC and SpdSyn."
- **What it does NOT contain:**
  - no residues or putrescine-site mapping (Glu178 is never mentioned);
  - no tree;
  - no gene transfer of AdoMetDC.
- **Useful for our paper:**
  1. **One more citation for the plant exception we already handle:** plants keep Glu but are not putrescine-activated, because built-in basic residues stand in for putrescine (Bennett 2002, already in step 5). This backs our wording that Glu178 is *needed for* putrescine activation, not *proof of* it.
  2. Its thermospermine synthase paragraph is a model for how a red-algal gene transfer into "Chromalveolata" is normally argued. That matters for us in one way: our AdoMetDC lineage does **not** follow that red-algal pattern, because red algae and haptophytes keep Glu and sit elsewhere in our tree.
- **Leads from its references:** Lee 1997 (carnation), Xiong 1997 (processing), Franceschetti 2001 (plant gene families) and Hanfrey 2005 (upstream-ORF circuit) are all already in our pool. **No new leads.**

### ✅ Green et al. 2011 *Mol Microbiol* 81:1109–1124, bacterial AdoMetDC–aminopropyltransferase fusions (PMID 21762220; PMC3196669, NIH author manuscript). **Verdict: does not pre-empt us.** *(Main text read in full 6 Nov; supplement S1–S7 read 6 Nov from your PDF.)*

- **What it shows:**
  - Bacterial **class-1b** AdoMetDC–aminopropyltransferase fusions are spread across many phyla. Some formed independently and some moved by gene transfer. They make spermidine from putrescine.
  - The *Pelagibacter* fusion AdoMetDC is stimulated by putrescine (20.7 → 174.6 nmol CO₂/h/mg with 20 mM putrescine; Table 1). **This is a bacterial class-1b enzyme, not our class-2 site, so it doesn't bear on Glu178.**
  - Archaeal agmatine-deiminase–NCPAH fusions.
- **The only eukaryotic class-2 content (one paragraph, in Results):**
  - Two *Micromonas* green algae carry a **class-2 AdoMetDC fused to ODC**: *M. pusilla* CCMP1545 EEH58717 (1,019 aa) and *Micromonas* sp. RCC299 XP_002502359 (1,039 aa).
  - The AdoMetDC part is at the N-terminus and the ODC part at the C-terminus. The mRNA has a small upstream ORF, like *Ostreococcus* AdoMetDC.
  - It is an **independent fusion from *Plasmodium*'s**, because both parts most resemble the green-algal/plant genes.
- **What it does NOT contain:**
  - no class-2 tree (its trees, Fig. 2 and Fig. S6, cover only the class-1b fusion domains);
  - no putrescine site / Glu178;
  - no stramenopile, cryptophyte, euglenid or dinoflagellate AdoMetDC;
  - **the main text names only two eukaryotic AdoMetDC–ODC fusions: *Plasmodium* and *Micromonas*.**
- **My own check (6 Nov):** *Micromonas* RCC299 XP_002502359 = UniProt **C1E5E7** (1,039 aa; Pfam PF01536 + ODC domains PF02784/PF00278). It is in your FOLDTRACE list but not in our 339-enzyme set.
  - Pairwise MAFFT of its first 520 aa to human P17707 gives 36% identity. Readout:
    - **Glu kept at 178** (E300);
    - processing site S68–S69 conserved;
    - **Arg at the human L13 position and Lys at F111**, the "built-in basic residue" pattern Bennett 2002 describes for plants.
  - So it fits our green-algae row (Glu kept). *Caveat: this is a single pairwise alignment, not our MSA pipeline.*
- **Useful for our paper:**
  1. Our dinoflagellate copy that is often fused to ODC (the Gln copy that sits with apicomplexans) **is not described here**. The main text knows only *Plasmodium* and *Micromonas*.
  2. **Before we call the dinoflagellate fusion "new", I still need:** (a) this paper's Fig. S7, which it says shows "all gene fusions involved in polyamine biosynthesis discovered to date"; (b) a search for any later report.
     - Since *Plasmodium* (an apicomplexan) has the same arrangement, the dinoflagellate fusion may simply be inherited from their shared ancestor. That would make it background, not a finding. **Logged as an open question, not a claim.**
- **Supplement Figs S1–S7 (your PDF, 6 Nov): checked, all bacterial/archaeal.** S6 and S7 viewed as images; S1–S5 read from their text and legends.
  - **S1:** alignment of 20 bacterial fusion proteins.
  - **S2–S3:** marine-metagenome *Pelagibacter*-type fusions.
  - **S4–S5:** gene layouts and junctions (class-1a pairs, archaeal NCPAH–AIH fusions, readthrough examples).
  - **S6:** neighbour-joining tree of bacterial class-1b AdoMetDCs only (200 bootstraps).
  - **S7:** a generic pathway sketch that shades *which steps* have fusions (ODC+AdoMetDC is one of them). **It names no organisms.**
  - **Not one eukaryotic sequence appears in the supplement.**
  - **So Green 2011 does not report a dinoflagellate ODC–AdoMetDC fusion.** Before calling ours new, a quick PubMed/literature search is still needed. And it may simply be inherited from the ancestor dinoflagellates share with *Plasmodium*.

### ✅ Illingworth et al. 2003 *FEBS Lett* 549:26–30, "The diverse bacterial origins of the Arabidopsis polyamine biosynthetic pathway" (PMID 12914918), 5 pp. **Verdict: does not pre-empt us. There is no tree in it.**

  - **The *Shewanella* claim is one sentence:** *S. oneidensis* NP_717479 resembles human and *Arabidopsis* AdoMetDC. Because it is "the single instance … in one bacterial species", the gene "was more probably captured by horizontal transfer from a eukaryotic source".

### ✅ Li et al. 2019 *Biochem J* 476(18):2579–2594, "A polyamine-independent role for S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase" (PMID 31467246; DOI 10.1042/BCJ20190561), 37-page accepted manuscript. **Verdict: does not pre-empt us, but we must cite it and answer its tree.**
*(Your PDF, 7 Nov. It is the accepted manuscript, not the final typeset version. I removed the line numbers and read every section in order: abstract, intro, methods, results, discussion, all 9 figure legends, and the reference list. **Fig. 2 (their class-2 tree) viewed at high resolution.** Supplementary Fig. S2 (class-2 alignment) read column by column. Figs S1, S3–S5 read from legends and text: class-1 alignment, HPLC, spermidine-synthase alignment, membrane prediction.)*
- **What it shows:**
  - "Eukaryotic" class-2 AdoMetDC exists in 257 bacterial genomes, mostly γ-proteobacteria (176 in Alteromonadales, e.g. *Shewanella*). Four of them work in *E. coli*.
  - The bacterial copies form one group *inside* the eukaryote tree, so they came from a single eukaryotic source. They conclude class 2 is "a eukaryotic invention", present before the last common ancestor of eukaryotes.
